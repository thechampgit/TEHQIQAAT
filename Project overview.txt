# TEHQIQAAT — Project Overview

## 1. Introduction

TEHQIQAAT is an AI-powered, agentic identity and travel document screening platform designed to assist authorized personnel in the verification and preliminary investigation of identity and travel documents.

The system is designed for high-volume environments such as border checkpoints, immigration counters, airports, and other identity-verification settings where officers may need to examine large numbers of passports, visas, national identity documents, permits, and related records.

Instead of treating document screening as a fixed sequence of operations, TEHQIQAAT introduces an **AI Screening Agent** that can dynamically determine which verification action should be performed next based on the evidence available at each stage.

The system combines document intelligence, OCR, computer vision, face verification, consistency analysis, and AI-based decision orchestration to produce an explainable screening result.

> TEHQIQAAT — Investigate. Verify. Assess.

---

## 2. Problem Statement

Border and identity checkpoints process a large volume of identity and travel documents every day. These documents may include:

* Passports
* Visas
* National identity cards
* Driving licences
* Residence permits
* Travel authorizations
* Supporting identity documents

Manual inspection of these documents can be time-consuming and dependent on the experience of individual officers.

Potential problems include:

* Forged documents
* Altered photographs
* Modified personal information
* Tampered dates or document numbers
* Forged visa stamps
* Identity impersonation
* Inconsistent information across documents
* Expired documents
* Suspicious or incomplete records

Sophisticated manipulation can also be difficult to identify through visual inspection alone.

A conventional verification system may execute a predefined sequence of checks regardless of the results obtained during earlier stages. This can lead to unnecessary processing for straightforward cases while providing insufficient investigation for suspicious cases.

TEHQIQAAT addresses this problem by introducing an adaptive investigation layer that determines which verification operations are relevant to the current case.

---

## 3. Proposed Solution

TEHQIQAAT provides a centralized screening interface where an authorized user can submit identity and travel documents along with the information required for verification.

The system first establishes the screening objective and analyzes the available evidence.

The AI Screening Agent then coordinates specialized tools for tasks such as:

1. Document classification
2. OCR and information extraction
3. MRZ extraction and parsing
4. Document validation
5. Tampering detection
6. Photo-forensic analysis
7. Face verification
8. Cross-document consistency checking
9. Risk assessment
10. Report generation

The agent does not necessarily execute every operation for every case.

Instead, it evaluates intermediate results and determines what action is appropriate next.

For example:

```text
Document submitted
        ↓
OCR extraction
        ↓
Low OCR confidence
        ↓
Agent detects unreliable result
        ↓
Image enhancement selected
        ↓
OCR executed again
        ↓
Reliable information obtained
        ↓
Validation
        ↓
Inconsistency detected
        ↓
Cross-document verification selected
        ↓
Evidence evaluated
        ↓
Risk assessment
        ↓
Human review if required
```

This adaptive behavior is a core part of the TEHQIQAAT architecture.

## 4. Why an Agentic System?

A major objective of TEHQIQAAT is to demonstrate how an agentic architecture can improve a traditionally sequential verification workflow.

A conventional pipeline may look like:

```text
OCR
 ↓
Validation
 ↓
Tampering Detection
 ↓
Face Verification
 ↓
Risk Score
```

The same sequence is executed for most cases regardless of the intermediate results.

TEHQIQAAT instead uses a goal-oriented agent:

```text
                 ┌─────────────────────┐
                 │   Screening Goal     │
                 └──────────┬──────────┘
                            ↓
                 ┌─────────────────────┐
                 │   Screening Agent   │
                 └──────────┬──────────┘
                            ↓
                 Observe Current Evidence
                            ↓
                 Select Appropriate Tool
                            ↓
                       Execute Tool
                            ↓
                 Evaluate Intermediate Result
                            ↓
                 ┌──────────┴──────────┐
                 ↓                     ↓
            Continue                 Adapt
                 ↓                     ↓
             Next Check       Retry / Different Tool
                 └──────────┬──────────┘
                            ↓
                    Risk Assessment
                            ↓
                    Human Review
```

The agent demonstrates five important agentic properties.

### 4.1 Goal-Driven Execution

The agent works toward a defined objective:

> Determine whether the submitted identity and travel documents can be verified reliably or whether the case requires further investigation or human review.

The system therefore does not simply execute isolated AI models. Individual tools contribute toward a larger screening objective.

### 4.2 Dynamic Action Selection

The agent selects tools according to the current evidence.

For example:

* High-quality document → normal OCR may be sufficient.
* Low OCR confidence → image enhancement and OCR retry.
* Suspicious photograph → photo-forensic analysis.
* Identity inconsistency → cross-document verification.
* Possible impersonation → face verification.
* Unresolved anomaly → human review.

### 4.3 Multi-Step Execution

A single screening case can require multiple dependent operations.

The result of one operation can determine the next operation.

For example:

```text
OCR
 ↓
Extract passport details
 ↓
Compare with visa
 ↓
Detect date inconsistency
 ↓
Investigate relevant documents
 ↓
Analyze photograph
 ↓
Perform face verification
 ↓
Generate risk assessment
```

### 4.4 Adaptation

The agent can modify its workflow when new information becomes available.

If an OCR operation produces an unreliable result, the agent does not have to treat that result as final.

It can instead:

```text
OCR → Low Confidence
         ↓
Image Enhancement
         ↓
OCR Retry
         ↓
Improved Confidence
```

Similarly, a suspicious result can cause the agent to perform additional checks.

### 4.5 Robustness

The system is designed to handle incomplete or failed operations.

Possible responses include:

* Retrying a failed operation
* Selecting an alternative processing method
* Continuing with available evidence
* Marking a result as uncertain
* Escalating the case for human review

This prevents a single failed AI component from automatically terminating the entire screening workflow.

## 6. Core Features

### Document Intelligence

The system can process different types of identity and travel documents and identify the relevant document type before applying document-specific extraction and validation logic.

### OCR-Based Information Extraction

Important fields can be extracted from documents, including:

* Name
* Document number
* Nationality
* Date of birth
* Gender
* Issue date
* Expiry date
* Visa number
* Visa type
* Entry validity
* Stay duration

The exact fields depend on the document type.

### MRZ Processing

Machine Readable Zones present in passports and other supported documents can be extracted and parsed to obtain standardized identity information.

### Document Validation

Extracted information can be checked against configurable validation rules.

Examples include:

* Required field presence
* Date validity
* Expiration status
* Document number format
* MRZ consistency
* Internal field consistency

### Tampering Detection

Computer-vision and image-forensic techniques can be used to identify indications of document manipulation.

Potential analysis areas include:

* Photograph replacement
* Text-region manipulation
* Inconsistent image regions
* Suspicious compression patterns
* Altered document elements
* Potentially forged stamps

These results should be treated as indicators requiring further assessment rather than absolute proof of forgery.

### Face Verification

The system can compare a face presented during screening with the photograph contained in an identity document.

The purpose is to identify potential identity mismatch or impersonation.

### Cross-Document Verification

Information extracted from multiple documents can be compared.

For example:

```text
Passport
Name: A
DOB: X
Passport No.: P

        VS

Visa
Name: A
DOB: Y
Visa No.: V
```

A mismatch in relevant fields can trigger additional investigation.

### Risk Assessment

Evidence collected during screening can be combined into a configurable risk assessment.

The system can present:

* Overall risk level
* Contributing factors
* Detected inconsistencies
* Verification results
* Confidence information
* Actions performed by the agent
* Recommended next action

### Explainable Screening Report

Instead of returning only a numerical score, TEHQIQAAT records the evidence and processing steps that contributed to the result.

This allows an authorized officer to understand **why** the system reached a particular screening assessment.

## 8. Screening Agent

The Screening Agent is the central orchestration component of TEHQIQAAT.

It maintains the state of the current screening case and uses the available evidence to determine the next appropriate action.

Conceptually, the agent maintains information such as:

```text
Case State
├── Documents submitted
├── Document types
├── Extracted fields
├── OCR confidence
├── Validation results
├── Tampering indicators
├── Face verification results
├── Cross-document inconsistencies
├── Tools already executed
├── Failed operations
└── Current risk assessment
```

The agent uses this state when selecting subsequent actions.

For example, if OCR has already successfully extracted all required information, repeating OCR would provide little value. If the extracted information reveals an inconsistency, however, the agent may select a cross-document analysis tool.

## 9. Example Screening Scenario

Consider a passenger who submits a passport and visa.

The workflow may proceed as follows:

```text
1. Screening goal established
          ↓
2. Passport classified
          ↓
3. Passport OCR performed
          ↓
4. Visa classified
          ↓
5. Visa OCR performed
          ↓
6. Passport and visa information compared
          ↓
7. Date-of-birth inconsistency detected
          ↓
8. Agent selects cross-document verification
          ↓
9. Inconsistency confirmed
          ↓
10. Photograph appears suspicious
          ↓
11. Agent selects additional image analysis
          ↓
12. Face verification performed
          ↓
13. Face similarity below configured threshold
          ↓
14. Evidence combined
          ↓
15. Elevated risk assessment generated
          ↓
16. Case escalated for human review
```

The important point is that the agent's later actions are influenced by earlier results.

## 10. Failure and Recovery

Real-world document images may be:

* Blurry
* Cropped
* Low-resolution
* Rotated
* Poorly illuminated
* Partially obstructed

TEHQIQAAT therefore treats tool outputs as potentially uncertain rather than assuming that every operation succeeds.

For example:

```text
OCR Attempt
    ↓
Confidence < Threshold
    ↓
Agent identifies unreliable result
    ↓
Image Enhancement
    ↓
OCR Retry
    ↓
Confidence improves
    ↓
Continue screening
```

If the system cannot obtain reliable evidence after reasonable recovery attempts, the case can be marked for human review rather than producing a false level of certainty.

---

## 11. Human-in-the-Loop Design

TEHQIQAAT is designed as a **decision-support system**, not an autonomous legal or immigration decision-maker.

The system can identify anomalies, organize evidence, calculate a configurable screening risk, and recommend additional verification.

However, final decisions remain with authorized personnel.

Human review may be triggered when:

* Evidence is contradictory
* AI confidence is low
* Important information cannot be extracted
* A potentially manipulated document is detected
* Face verification is inconclusive
* Processing repeatedly fails
* The case requires contextual judgment

This approach combines automated analysis with human oversight.

## 12. Expected Impact

TEHQIQAAT aims to provide the following benefits:

### Faster Screening

Automating repetitive extraction and verification tasks can reduce the time required for preliminary document analysis.

### Improved Consistency

Standardized validation and analysis can reduce variation caused by purely manual workflows.

### Adaptive Investigation

Suspicious or uncertain cases can receive additional analysis instead of being processed identically to straightforward cases.

### Explainable Results

The system can provide evidence and processing history rather than only returning a final score.

Officers can focus their attention on cases requiring human judgment while routine analysis is handled automatically.

## 13. Limitations

TEHQIQAAT is a prototype and has important limitations.

AI-based document analysis cannot guarantee that a document is genuine or fraudulent.

OCR may fail on poor-quality images. Computer-vision models may produce false positives or false negatives. Face verification performance can vary according to image quality, pose, lighting, and other factors.

Similarly, a risk score represents an automated assessment based on available evidence and configurable rules. It should not be interpreted as a legal determination.

Therefore, the system is intended to **assist authorized personnel rather than replace them**.

---

## 14. Security and Privacy Considerations

Identity and travel documents contain sensitive personal information.

A production implementation of TEHQIQAAT would therefore require appropriate controls including:

* Encryption of data in transit and at rest
* Secure authentication and authorization
* Role-based access control
* Minimal data retention
* Audit logging
* Secure API communication
* Controlled access to uploaded documents
* Protection of biometric information
* Appropriate deletion and retention policies

For the hackathon prototype, sample or synthetic documents should be preferred wherever possible.

## 16. Conclusion

TEHQIQAAT proposes an agentic approach to identity and travel document screening in which AI does more than execute a predefined sequence of models.
The system establishes a screening objective, analyzes available evidence, dynamically selects verification tools, evaluates intermediate results, adapts when operations fail or suspicious evidence appears, and produces an explainable screening assessment.

By combining document intelligence, computer vision, verification tools, adaptive agent orchestration, and human oversight, TEHQIQAAT aims to demonstrate how agentic AI can support faster, more consistent, and evidence-driven identity document screening.
